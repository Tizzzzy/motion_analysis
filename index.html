For law students & young attorneys · Client-centered motion practice

Write better motions through conversational guidance.

Great motions start long before you type a caption. This workspace coaches you through pre-meeting preparation, a live simulated client interview, and post-meeting Rule 12(b)(6) & Illinois contract analysis — evaluating every step against the course rubric before you draft.

Why conversational guidance instead of a "win likelihood" score? Legal-judgment-prediction tools often rely on outcome-derived signals unavailable when the filing decision actually had to be made (Medvedeva & McBride). Instead of guessing how a judge will rule, our Qwen 3.8 mentor evaluates authority recognition, client-interest prioritization, pleading-versus-proof discipline, and strategic judgment using only legitimate pre-filing information.

Phase 1 · Mentor Q&A

Before Meeting

Start with "Who is the client and who can authorize decisions?" Frame the alleged contract claim, identify missing documents, and weigh what a motion would achieve or cost.

Phase 2 · Client Roleplay

During Meeting

Roles switch: interview the simulated client directly to elicit ranked outcomes, budget limits, deadlines, settlement terms, relational stakes, adverse facts, and sign-off rules.

Phase 3 · Mentor Debrief

After Meeting

Synthesize client priorities, apply Illinois breach-of-contract law and the 7th Circuit Rule 12(b)(6) Twombly/Iqbal framework, compare alternatives, and form a recommendation.

Step 4 · Drafting Studio

Motion Drafting

Carry your interview insights and rubric scorecard into the drafting editor to write your Rule 12(b)(6) motion or strategic counseling memo and receive AI partner feedback.
