Motion Analysis — Interactive Legal Case Analysis & Strategy Simulation

Motion Analysis is an interdisciplinary learning platform that helps law students practice legal analysis, client counseling, and strategic decision-making through interactive civil litigation scenarios. Focusing initially on Illinois breach-of-contract disputes in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of Illinois, students investigate case facts, interview simulated clients, evaluate federal motion standards, assess costs and uncertainty, and explain recommendations in light of client goals. The platform supports Rule 12(b)(6) pleading analysis and, with an appropriate evidentiary record, Rule 56 summary judgment analysis. Feedback emphasizes the quality of students’ legal and strategic reasoning.

Core Learning Objective: Students learn to discover hidden client priorities, compare legally defensible strategies (Negotiate, Litigate 12(b)(6), Investigate, and post-discovery Rule 56), anticipate opposing-party reactions, quantify uncertainty through conditional probability trees, experience a simulated real-world event, and explain or revise their recommendation against an official 100-Point Faculty Rubric.

Legal Standards & Federal Rules Architecture

Default Jurisdiction: N.D. Ill. (Eastern Division)

Core Doctrinal Distinction: Keep Rule 12(b)(6) and Rule 56 separate — pleading sufficiency at the initial appearance is fundamentally different from an evidentiary determination about genuine disputes of material fact.

Rule 12(b)(6) Pleading Standard Initial Stage

Confined strictly to the Four Corners of the complaint (and contracts incorporated by reference). Under Twombly and Iqbal, factual allegations are accepted as true, but conclusory labels are disregarded. In the Seventh Circuit, district courts routinely grant leave to amend upon dismissal (Runnion v. Girl Scouts, 786 F.3d 510).

Key focus: Does the plaintiff state a plausible claim for breach of contract under Illinois law?
Rule 56 Summary Judgment Evidentiary Stage

Evaluated only after an appropriate evidentiary record exists (affidavits, depositions, sensor logs, carrier inspection records). Granted if there is no genuine dispute as to any material fact and the movant is entitled to judgment as a matter of law (Celotex, Anderson, Swyear). Reasonable inferences are drawn in favor of the nonmovant.

Key focus: Can either party prove or disprove breach, causation, or liability caps without factfinder resolution?
N.D. Ill. Local Rule 56.1 Local Practice

Mandates numbered statements of material facts with specific record citations. The supplied April 20, 2006 text (80 moving facts / 40 opposing facts) is provided as historical reference; current rules and judge-specific standing orders must be verified for active practice.

The 6-Stage Student Experience & Branching Model

Grounded in our closed-world prototype: Heartland Specialty Foods, Inc. v. Lakeview Cold Chain Logistics, LLC (N.D. Ill.).

Stage 1 · Fact Discovery

1. Client Interview

Ask open-ended questions to simulated client representatives (CEO Elena Rostova and Warehouse Ops Director Marcus Vance). Uncover authority limits, budget constraints, and rank Lakeview's top 3 business priorities.

Stage 2 · Quantitative Analysis

2. Quantify Tradeoffs

Enter estimated legal fees, liability exposure, and conditional probabilities. Display expected financial values, client utility weighting, and sensitivity to 7th Circuit leave-to-amend odds.

Stage 3 · Strategic Empathy

3. Opponent Anticipation

Analyze Heartland's commercial pressures, supplier debts, and litigation incentives. Anticipate opposing counsel's response to an early settlement offer versus a Rule 12(b)(6) motion.

Stage 4 · Strategy Formulation

4. Initial Recommendation

Commit to a defensible course of action: Structured Settlement (Option A), Rule 12(b)(6) Motion (Option B), or Pre-Answer Investigation (Option C). Write a detailed strategic rationale tied to client goals.

Stage 5 · Dynamic Scenario

5. Experience an Event

Experience a scripted, controlled court ruling or opponent letter. Discover how 7th Circuit Rule 15 standards and commercial loan refinancing deadlines disrupt reflexive motion practice.

Stage 6 · Formative Evaluation

6. Reflect & Revise

Compare pre-event assumptions with post-event reality. Explain whether your recommendation pivots or persists. Receive an automated 100-point faculty rubric evaluation across 5 dimensions.
