For new attorneys & law students

Before you write the motion, understand why you're filing it.

Most young attorneys draft a motion because a partner told them to — not because they've worked through what the motion is actually supposed to accomplish. This tool walks you through that thinking first, then helps you analyze your draft sentence by sentence and estimates how a judge is likely to rule.

How this works

1
Walk through your mindset Answer a short series of strategic questions — your purpose, the judge's likely reaction, your opponent's response, and what happens if you lose.
2
Review your reflection See all of your answers together. This is the thinking that should drive how you write the motion — not the other way around.
3
Draft and analyze your motion Paste your draft. Get sentence-by-sentence reasoning and an estimated likelihood the motion is granted, grounded in what you said you were trying to accomplish.
