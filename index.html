Strategic Judgment Simulation: Commercial Litigation & Client Counseling

A controlled, interactive Illinois breach-of-contract hypothetical that develops students' strategic judgment. The project is not primarily a motion-outcome predictor, case-upload analyzer, or litigation-procedure tutorial. Students learn that a legally defensible argument may still be a poor strategic choice for a particular client.

Core Learning Objective: Students learn to discover hidden client priorities, compare legally defensible strategies (Negotiate, Litigate 12(b)(6), Investigate), anticipate opposing-party reactions, quantify uncertainty through conditional probability trees, experience a simulated real-world event, and explain or revise their recommendation against an official 100-Point Faculty Rubric.

The 6-Stage Student Experience & Branching Model

Grounded in our closed-world prototype: Heartland Specialty Foods, Inc. v. Lakeview Cold Chain Logistics, LLC (N.D. Ill.).

Stage 1 · Fact Discovery

1. Client Interview

Ask open-ended questions to simulated client representatives (CEO Elena Rostova and Warehouse Ops Director Marcus Vance). Uncover authority limits, budget constraints, and rank Lakeview's top 3 business priorities.

Stage 2 · Quantitative Analysis

2. Quantify Tradeoffs

Enter estimated legal fees, liability exposure, and conditional probabilities. Display expected financial values, client utility weighting, and sensitivity to 7th Circuit leave-to-amend odds.

Stage 3 · Strategic Empathy

3. Opponent Anticipation

Analyze Heartland's commercial pressures, supplier debts, and litigation incentives. Anticipate opposing counsel's response to an early settlement offer versus a Rule 12(b)(6) motion.

Stage 4 · Strategy Formulation

4. Initial Recommendation

Commit to a defensible course of action: Structured Settlement (Option A), Rule 12(b)(6) Motion (Option B), or Pre-Answer Investigation (Option C). Write a detailed strategic rationale tied to client goals.

Stage 5 · Dynamic Scenario

5. Experience an Event

Experience a scripted, controlled court ruling or opponent letter. Discover how 7th Circuit Rule 15 standards and commercial loan refinancing deadlines disrupt reflexive motion practice.

Stage 6 · Formative Evaluation

6. Reflect & Revise

Compare pre-event assumptions with post-event reality. Explain whether your recommendation pivots or persists. Receive an automated 100-point faculty rubric evaluation across 5 dimensions.

Prototype Case: Heartland Specialty Foods v. Lakeview Cold Chain Logistics

Forum: N.D. Ill. (Eastern Division)
Parties: Wisconsin Dairy Producer v. Illinois Warehouse LLC
Claimed Damages: $68,400 direct inventory + $340,000 lost profits
Critical Constraint: 75-Day Bank Refinancing Review Window

Marcus Vance wants to file an immediate motion to dismiss. As outside counsel, you must interview CEO Elena Rostova, discover that Lakeview's credit facility will be frozen if litigation is unresolved after 75 days, analyze Section 11.2's limitation of liability, and advise the client on the best path forward.

Faculty Evaluation Rubric (100 Points Total)

Dimension Points What Earns Credit
1. Client Interviewing 25 pts Relevant open-ended follow-ups; uncovers client objectives, 75-day refinancing deadline, budget limits, and Elena Rostova's authority vs CFO sign-off.
2. Legal Reasoning 25 pts Accurate application of Illinois contract elements (Zahdan), Master Agreement Sec. 11.2 limitation of liability, and 7th Circuit Rule 12(b)(6) / Rule 15 pleading doctrine (Runnion).
3. Risk and Uncertainty 20 pts Justifies estimates; distinguishes binding legal rules from empirical observations and scenario assumptions; calculates conditional probabilities; explores sensitivity.
4. Strategic Judgment 20 pts Compares defensible alternatives (Negotiate vs Litigate vs Investigate); ties recommendation to client business goals; resists reflexive motion filing.
5. Reflection and Revision 10 pts Responds thoughtfully to simulated court order or opponent counter; explains changes or persistence; compares initial vs revised reasoning.
