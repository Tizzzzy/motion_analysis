What information this analysis uses — and why

Every factor here was first run through a two-step test: was it available to the attorney before the decision to file had to be made (temporal legitimacy), and if so, is it appropriate to use given what this tool is trying to teach (pedagogical legitimacy)?

Used in this analysis

  • Allegations in the complaint
  • Claim type you select (Torts, Contracts, Property)
  • Rule 12(b)(6) pleading standard
  • Twombly v. Iqbal plausibility standard
  • Court/district
  • Other controlling precedent as of filing
  • Procedural posture / docket history

Available before filing, and directly relevant to the pleading standard.

Deliberately set aside

  • Assigned judge & their grant/denial history
  • Timing of the motion
  • Party or attorney/firm characteristics

Technically available before filing, but using judge-specific tendencies here would shift the focus from legal merit to predicting an individual judge — a separate question this tool isn't trying to answer.

Never used to generate this analysis

  • The eventual ruling or final opinion
  • Facts or language first appearing in the court's decision
  • Anything occurring after the filing decision (later docket entries, evidence, appeals)

Using outcome-derived facts to "predict" the outcome isn't genuine prediction — it's restating the answer in advance. The ruling is reserved for feedback after you've already decided.
